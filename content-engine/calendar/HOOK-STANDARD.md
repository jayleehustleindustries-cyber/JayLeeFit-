# HOOK STANDARD — brand voice as rules

This file is the brand DNA of Everyday Hustle Co. hooks, written so a
model with no taste can draft and verify them. If a hook "feels right"
but fails a rule here, the rule wins. If a rule here produces bad hooks,
Aaron changes the rule — models don't.

## 1. Universal rules (every hook, every type)

U1. **≤ 15 words** total. Count them. Contractions ("don't") = 1 word.
U2. **Declarative statements only.** No questions. No "imagine…". No
    "What if…".
U3. **No emojis, no hashtags, no exclamation points, no ALL-CAPS words.**
U4. **No numbers, percentages, studies, credentials, client results, or
    guarantees.** ("Most folded by Tuesday" is fine — it's rhetoric, not
    a statistic. "87% of lifters…" is banned.) This is the claims
    standard; violating it BLOCKS the row.
U5. **No direct advice verbs aimed at the viewer**: never "you should",
    "make sure", "try", "remember to", "here's how". Hooks state; the
    video teaches.
U6. **Banned phrases** (exact or near-match, case-insensitive):
    beast mode · no pain no gain · rise and grind · crush your workout ·
    crush your goals · smash your goals · you got this · believe in
    yourself · level up · game changer · game-changer · unlock ·
    unleash · secret to · hack(s) · top 5 · stop doing this ·
    doctors hate · transform your body · summer body · gym motivation ·
    let's go · day in the life · consistency is key
U7. **No repetition**: a hook may not reuse a 4+ word phrase from the
    previous two weeks' hooks, and Mechanical hooks may not target an
    exercise used in the previous two weeks. ONE exemption: the fixed
    skeleton of the Sunday V4 pattern ("…the standard held. Most … by
    …") repeats by design — for V4, only the variable parts (quit-verb,
    weekday) must differ from the previous two weeks.

## 2. Type definitions (the only three types)

### Mechanical (persona: Marie, always)
A form-mistake teaching hook. It must contain BOTH:
(a) a named exercise, and (b) a wrong-outcome claim — what the mistake
actually trains or does instead of the intended target.
Tone: dry, surgical, slightly amused. Marie never insults the viewer;
she insults the rep.

### Villain (persona: Jay, always)
An identity declaration. Jay does not motivate the viewer — he states
his own standard and lets the contrast do the work. Structure is always
a contrast: motivation vs. standard, wanting vs. paying, dark vs. light,
most people vs. us. First person ("I", "we") or brand-voice absolute.
Never mentions the viewer's goals. Never explains.

### Filter (persona: Jay, always; Fridays only; exactly 1 per week)
A statement designed to repel the wrong audience. MUST contain an
explicit exclusion: "not for you", "isn't for you", "we're not for you",
"this page will disappoint you", or equivalent. Conditional form:
"If <casual-audience behavior>, <exclusion>."

## 3. Pattern banks (draft ONLY from these)

Each hook must instantiate one pattern below. Fill the slots; don't
invent new sentence shapes. Mark the pattern ID in the row's Pattern
column. **Do not use the same pattern ID twice in one week**, and do not
use a pattern ID that appears in either of the previous two weeks
(3-week cooldown; the Pattern column in `weeks/` files is how you
check). The slot-bound patterns **V3 (Sat) and V4 (Sun) are exempt from
the cooldown** — they run every week by design; their freshness comes
from changing the words, not the shape.

### Mechanical patterns (Mon, Wed)
- M1: "Your <exercise> is a <different exercise> <absurd qualifier>."
  — gold: "Your lat pulldown is a bicep curl with a cable bolted to the ceiling."
- M2: "<Flawed-version exercise> grow(s) your <wrong muscle>, not your <target muscle>."
  — gold: "Half-rep lateral raises grow your traps, not your delts."
- M3: "<Cheat movement> builds <byproduct>, not <target muscle>."
  — e.g.: "Swinging dumbbell curls builds momentum, not biceps."
- M4: "The moment <form break>, your <exercise> becomes a <lesser exercise>."
- M5: "<Exercise> doesn't train <target> if <form break>."
- Fallback (evergreen, use only per SOP §5): M2 with an exercise from
  the approved claims list below that hasn't run in 8 weeks.

**Approved mechanical claims list** (a weak model may ONLY build
Mechanical hooks from these, because they are verifiably true; Aaron
extends this list — models never add to it):
1. Lat pulldown pulled with arms/biceps instead of driving elbows down → biceps do the work, lats don't.
2. Lateral raises with shrugged shoulders / partial range → traps take over from delts.
3. Swinging/cheating dumbbell curls → momentum robs the biceps of tension.
4. Rows with shoulders rolling forward / no scapular retraction → rear delts and arms take over from mid-back.
5. Squats cut high above parallel → less glute/quad stimulus than full range.
6. Bench press bounced off the chest → stretch reflex does the bottom work, pecs don't.
7. Deadlift jerked with a rounded lower back → spinal loading substitutes for hip drive.
8. Hip thrusts without full lockout → hamstrings dominate, glutes underloaded.
9. Overhead press with excessive back lean → becomes an incline press, front delts underworked.
10. Pull-ups kipping/half range → lats never reach full stretch or contraction.

### Villain patterns (Tue, Thu, Sat, Sun)
- V1 (identity): "I don't <common crutch>. I <harder replacement>."
  — gold: "I don't chase motivation. I bury it and build on top of it."
- V2 (defiance): "<The obstacle> doesn't know what hit it. Neither will <the excuse/the doubt>."
  — gold: "The weights don't know what hit them. Neither will your excuses."
- V3 (aesthetic, Sat slot): "<Past participle> in the <hard place>. <Past participle> in the <visible place>." ≤9 words, two clauses.
  — gold: "Built in the dark. Worn in the light."
- V4 (recap, Sun slot): "<Duration> the standard held. Most <quit-verb> by <early weekday>."
  — gold: "Another week the standard held. Most folded by Tuesday."
  — vary the quit-verb (folded, negotiated, bargained, rescheduled,
    "made peace") and the weekday; never reuse last week's pair.
- V5 (cost): "Everyone wants <the result>. Nobody wants <the boring price>."
- V6 (standard): "Motivation <weak behavior>. The standard <doesn't/won't>."
- Fallback: V1 with a crutch not used in the last 8 weeks (crutch bank:
  motivation, playlists, pre-workout, perfect conditions, rest-day math,
  new-program energy, January).

### Filter patterns (Fri only)
- F1: "If you need <crutch> to <train/show up>, this brand isn't for you."
  — gold: "If you need motivation to train, this brand isn't for you."
- F2: "If your <commitment object> changes every time <trivial trigger>, we're not for you."
- F3: "<Soft-audience habit> is fine. Just not here."
- Fallback: F1 with a crutch from the V-fallback crutch bank.

## 4. Verification checklist (pass/fail per row — run in Step 3)

For EACH of the 7 rows, check all boxes. Any unchecked box = FAIL =
rewrite the row. No exceptions, no "close enough."

- [ ] Word count ≤ 15 (count them one by one)
- [ ] It is a statement — contains no "?"
- [ ] Zero emojis, hashtags, "!" characters, ALL-CAPS words
- [ ] Zero digits/percentages/statistics/credentials/outcome claims (U4)
- [ ] None of the U6 banned phrases appear (string-search each one)
- [ ] Type matches the fixed day rotation in SOP §3 Step 2
- [ ] Persona matches type (Mechanical→Marie; Villain/Filter→Jay)
- [ ] Pattern ID is named, comes from §3, is unique this week, and does
      not appear in the previous two `weeks/` files (3-week cooldown;
      V3/V4 exempt)
- [ ] Mechanical only: exercise named + wrong-outcome claim present, and
      the claim is one of the 10 approved claims (by number)
- [ ] Mechanical only: exercise not used in previous two weeks (U7)
- [ ] Villain only: contains a contrast (two opposed halves) and no
      viewer-advice verbs (U5)
- [ ] Sat only: ≤ 9 words, exactly two clauses
- [ ] Filter only (Fri): contains an explicit exclusion clause (§2)
- [ ] No 4+ word phrase shared with the previous two weeks' hooks (U7)

Whole-draft checks:
- [ ] Exactly 7 rows, Mon–Sun, dates correct for the target week
- [ ] Exactly 2 Mechanical, 4 Villain, 1 Filter
- [ ] Mon and Wed target different body parts
- [ ] Every row has Platform, Asset Need, Caption Angle, CTA filled
      (never blank — `Generate: …` is the allowed placeholder for assets)
- [ ] Alternates section present (1 per day)
- [ ] Footer line `Self-check: 7/7 PASS (<date>)` written only after all
      of the above are true

## 5. Keeping the banks alive (monthly, the one taste-requiring job)

Pattern banks go stale. On the first weekend of each month, the
strongest available model (or Aaron) does ONE thing: propose 2 new
patterns per type and 3 new approved mechanical claims, as a PR/edit to
this file. Aaron approves or rejects. Weak models running the weekly
loop NEVER edit this file — they only read it. Retire any pattern used
3+ times in the last 8 weeks by marking it `(resting until <date>)`.

## 6. Known failure modes (what bad output looks like)

1. **Generic gym-fluff**: "Push yourself harder every single day." —
   fails U6-adjacent tone, no pattern, no contrast. The banks exist to
   prevent exactly this.
2. **Advice leakage**: "Stop swinging your curls — keep your elbows
   pinned." — fails U5 (advice verbs); Mechanical states the mistake's
   consequence, it doesn't coach in the hook.
3. **Fake stats**: "90% of lifters butcher the lat pulldown." — fails
   U4; this is the BLOCKED-status claims violation from the production
   pipeline doc.
4. **Motivational Jay**: "You have greatness inside you — go get it." —
   Jay never addresses the viewer's potential; fails §2 Villain
   definition (no contrast, viewer-directed).
5. **Double Filter**: two repelling hooks in one week reads as hostile
   instead of exclusive — the rotation caps Filter at Friday only.
6. **Sunday clone**: reusing "Another week the standard held. Most
   folded by Tuesday." verbatim — V4 requires the quit-verb/day pair to
   change weekly.
7. **Untrue mechanics**: "Leg press grows your calves, not your quads."
   — pattern-shaped but false; this is why Mechanical hooks may only use
   the numbered approved-claims list.
