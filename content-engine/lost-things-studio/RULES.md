# Lost Things Studio — non-negotiable production rules

Agent-readable. Every agent (showrunner, script, visual, voice, edit,
QA) loads this file before touching any Lost Things asset. These are
distilled verbatim-in-spirit from the Agent 5 production package; where
this file and that doc conflict, the Drive doc wins.

## Originality / legal

1. NEVER imitate Bluey — no canine designs, color language, house
   layouts, facial construction, backgrounds, actor voices, episode
   plots, or comedic tone.
2. NEVER reproduce Pencilmation's ruled-paper world or Alan Becker's
   stick-figure/computer-screen premise. Their *lessons* (silhouettes,
   escalation, world-driven conflict) are fair; their *assets* are not.
3. No stick figures, no ruled paper, no copied sets, no existing
   franchise elements in any generated image.
4. Voices are original AI performances. No imitation of any recognizable
   actor or protected character. AI-generated voices must be disclosed
   in channel description or credits.

## Visual locks (every image generation)

5. Same four characters every time — Patch (unmatched sock), Clicker
   (retired TV remote), Button (brass coat button), Crumb (cracker
   crumb) — same proportions, colors, wear marks, facial construction.
6. Same under-couch architecture: warm wood floor, blue-gray couch
   underside, fabric-fiber columns, lost-object structures. Warm
   household light above; teal, amber, rust, navy, dusty gold below.
7. Original stylized 2D family animation. Cinematic staging, no
   photorealism, no horror imagery.
8. No text inside scene images. Expressions readable at phone size.
9. 16:9 at 1280×720 minimum for pilot frames; 9:16 crops planned at
   composition time for Shorts/Reels, not as an afterthought.

## Production discipline

10. Never generate a whole episode in one video prompt. Clips are 4, 8,
    or 12 seconds from approved scene references only.
11. Cast sheet gets generated and APPROVED before any scene reference;
    scene references approved before any animation.
12. Reject any output with identity drift, bad anatomy, random text,
    warped objects, or off-brand style — rejection is the default, not
    the exception.
13. Every 5–12 seconds of runtime must contain a meaningful visual
    change, new information, escalation, reaction, or emotional beat.
14. The opening must present a problem within 2 seconds.
15. Narration occupies less than 20% of runtime and never explains what
    the visuals already say.

## Story gates (QA rejects if missing)

16. Clear beginning, middle, ending.
17. A character makes a choice.
18. One memorable visual event.
19. Story understandable with the sound off.

## Money & publishing gates

20. No publishing without human approval. Ever.
21. No account login, payment, or irreversible platform action by any
    agent.
22. Paid generation (credits) requires an explicit human go-ahead with a
    cost preview first.
23. Do not scale to a weekly series until the two test shorts show
    strong completion + rewatch data (§12 of the Drive doc).

## Platform compliance

24. Each video needs original commentary, transformation, or a distinct
    story arc — no repetitive mass-produced AI filler (YouTube
    monetization policy: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392).
25. Family content follows YouTube kids/family best practices
    (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223).
